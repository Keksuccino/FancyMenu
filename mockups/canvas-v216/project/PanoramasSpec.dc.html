Panoramas without the file explorer

A panorama is six images on the inside of a cube that slowly turns around you, like the background of Minecraft's title screen. Today it's a folder you build by hand: six files that need exact names, and a file you write yourself. The Panoramas window makes, changes and deletes them. It shows the cube unfolded, with a box for every side, takes dropped images and folders, and previews the panorama while you set its speed and its camera. It opens from the menu bar and from every place that picks a panorama.

What gets in the way
  • A panorama gets made outside the game: a folder in config/fancymenu/panoramas, a panorama folder inside it, and a properties.txt next to that.
  • The six images have to be PNG files called panorama_0.png to panorama_5.png. Nothing in the game says which number is which side.
  • One image that's missing or misnamed, and the whole panorama shows as the purple and black missing texture. Only the log names the file.
  • properties.txt is written by hand. A missing bracket or a missing type line, and the panorama doesn't load.
  • speed is a factor without a unit: 1 takes four minutes for one turn, and anything above 10 turns like 10.
  • angle doesn't say which way it tilts. A positive one looks down.
  • A new panorama or a changed file only shows up after Reload FancyMenu.
  • The name in properties.txt is what layouts ask for. Changing it leaves every background that showed the panorama with the missing texture, without a warning.
  • Choose Panorama lists the panoramas and previews one, but it can't make, change or delete any.

The window, piece by piece

The list
One row per panorama, sorted by name: the middle of its front, the name, and under it the size of its images and how long one turn takes. Sides that still need an image show in amber, and missing files in red.
The header
The name with Rename, and a line with the size of its images in pixels and on disk. Below: Duplicate, Export as Text and Delete.
Sides
The first thing in the details. The cube unfolded: Left, Front, Right and Back in a row, Top above Front and Bottom below it, so pictures that belong together sit next to each other. Each box shows its image and its name. A click opens Choose Image, the × that appears on a box takes its image off, and dragging a box onto another swaps them. Choose Images in the header picks several files at once.
The preview
The cube from the inside, drawn like the game draws it: same field of view, same angle, same speed. A side without an image is dark and shows its name. The line below has Play and Pause and says how long one turn takes. Dragging the preview looks around, and letting go lets the tilt glide back to the panorama's angle.
Camera
Four sliders with their value next to them: Speed with the time one turn takes, Field of View, Angle with up or down, and Start Rotation. Each one changes the preview while it's dragged, and a drag is one undo step.
Overlay
One optional image that's drawn over the whole panorama, like a vignette or a logo. Its box stands in the corner, apart from the six sides, and never shows up on the cube.
Dropping files
Minecraft only reports files once they're dropped, so the window can't show a target while they hover (rule 118). The note under Sides says up front that dropping works. A drop goes to the selected panorama, or makes a new one when none or several are selected. Right after, the new images are tagged New and the footer says what got set and what got left out, with Undo.
The question sheet
For dropped images whose names don't say their side. The images are listed on the left, the unfolded cube is on the right, and the marked image shows big below it. A click on a side puts the marked image there and marks the next one. Images without a side get left out, and the button says how many sides it sets.
Several selected
How many, and their size on disk in all, with Duplicate and Delete for all of them. Camera sets every selected panorama, and a value that differs shows as Mixed. Below, the selected panoramas with how many places use each one.

Make one, bring one in, use one

From six screenshots
1Drop the six screenshots onto the window. Their names are dates, so the sheet asks.
2Click Front for the first image, Right for the second, and so on. Set All 6 Sides makes the panorama.
3Type its name. Drag the preview around, and swap two sides if a seam shows.
4Set Speed and Angle while the preview turns. Save writes the folder and its files.
A folder someone made
1Drop the folder sunrise_bay onto the window, with nothing selected.
2Its files are called panorama_0 to panorama_5, so every side is set at once and tagged New.
3The panorama is called Sunrise Bay, after the folder. The footer says what was left out, with Undo.
4Save copies the images into FancyMenu's own folder.
Straight from the layout editor
1Right-click the layout and open Background, then Panorama. It lists every panorama.
2The pencil on a row opens the window on that panorama. New Panorama opens it with a new one.
3Change its sides or its camera. The layout's background behind the window follows every change.
4Save keeps it. A new panorama is in the submenu from then on, and gets picked there like the others.

Special cases it handles

  • A side whose file is gone shows as missing, in its box and on the cube. A card at the top says which sides, and a click on one picks a new image.
  • A panorama with fewer than six images shows as the missing texture in menus, like today. The note under Sides counts the sides to go, and the preview already shows the ones that are there.
  • Sides are square. An image that isn't gets stretched onto its side, like today, and its box shows a warning with its size.
  • PNG and JPEG work. Other files in a drop get left out, and the footer names them.
  • A dropped folder is also searched in its panorama folder, so a whole panorama from another pack works. With nothing selected, its properties.txt brings the name and the camera along.
  • Two files in a drop that claim the same side: the first one gets it, and the sheet asks about the other.
  • In the sheet, a side that already has an image shows it dimmed, and gets the new one instead.
  • Speed ends at 10, because a higher value doesn't turn faster today. A file with more keeps its number until the slider is moved.
  • With Speed at 0 the panorama stands still, and the preview has nothing to play or pause.
  • Looking around in the preview changes nothing. The direction stays where you let go, and the tilt goes back to Angle.
  • Removing an image deletes its copy with Save, and deleting a panorama deletes its folder with Save. Until then Undo brings them back.
  • The folder is named after the panorama, in lowercase with _ for spaces, and gets renamed with it.
  • The window never sets which panorama a place shows. That gets picked in the place itself, in its panorama submenu.
  • A copy gets copies of the images. Nothing uses it until it's picked somewhere.
  • The details have two columns: Sides on the left, the preview with Camera right under it on the right, and Used in below both. The sides, the preview and all four sliders fit on the screen together. In a narrow window the right column moves below Sides.

Changes beyond the UI All approved

The window above already uses these. The first four change how panoramas get saved, named and found, so each one is a rework rule of its own. The others are rules that were there before.

Panoramas are made in a window Approved, rule 165
The Panoramas window writes the folder in config/fancymenu/panoramas, its properties.txt and its images on Save, so nobody has to. The folder is named after the panorama and gets renamed with it. Old panoramas load as they are, and changes show without Reload FancyMenu.
FancyMenu names the files of the sides Approved, rule 166
An image that gets picked or dropped is copied into the panorama as panorama_0.png to panorama_5.png, or overlay.png, whatever it was called before. A JPEG gets saved as a PNG. Dropped files that already have one of these names find their place, one image dropped onto a box goes there, and a sheet asks about the rest.
Panoramas know where they're used Approved, rule 167
The index of rule 87 also collects panorama names: the Panorama background of layouts, Custom Menu Background Panorama and Pre-Load Resources. Used in, the Not Used filter, the rename sheet and the delete sheet read it, and renaming a panorama can update those places.
Panoramas are managed where they're picked Approved, rule 168
The panorama submenu of rule 133 gets a pencil on every row, then New Panorama and Manage Panoramas. All three open the window, and picking stays in the submenu. Customization in the menu bar gets a Panoramas entry in its Manage section.
Dropped files land where the pointer is Covered, rule 118
Nothing reacts while files hover. The window says up front that dropping works, an image dropped onto a box goes to that side, and right after a drop the new images are tagged New.
Numbers with a range are sliders Covered, rule 17
Speed, Field of View, Angle and Start Rotation all have a range, so each one is a slider with its value next to it, and a drag is one undo step.
Changes show right away Covered, rule 38
A screen that shows a panorama follows every change to its sides and its camera, and Cancel puts everything back.
Questions are sheets Covered, rules 134 and 140
Which side is each image?, the rename question and the delete question slide down from the title bar, each with a button named after what it does.
